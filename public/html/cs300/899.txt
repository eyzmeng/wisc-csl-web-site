@Override is an ANNOTATION [TUT-A]. It hints to the COMPILER
javac(1) [MAN-C] that your method overrides another method.

@see is a Javadoc TAG (introduction: [TAG-I] specification: [TAG-S]).
It indicates the role of certain chunks of text in your Javadoc and
instructs the javadoc(1) [MAN-D] GENERATOR to process them differently.

It might seem that @Override is also a Javadoc tag due to their
apparent similarity, but they serve two COMPLETELY DIFFERENT PURPOSES.
I guess javadoc(1) also looks at @Override and adds a section for the
overridden method, but I'm pretty sure the compiler does not look at
@see or any of the Javadoc tags (Javadoc and its tags are comments,
after all --- they do not affect code compilation or runtime execution;
@Override affects the former.)

Conclusion: ANNOTATIONS like @Override, @SuppressWarnings(...),
@Deprecated IN GENERAL do not go inside Javadoc.  They are NOT
tags, period.

[TUT-A]: https://docs.oracle.com/javase/tutorial/java/annotations/
[TAG-I]: https://docs.oracle.com/javase/1.5.0/
  docs/tooldocs/windows/javadoc.html#javadoctags
[TAG-S]: https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/17/
  docs/specs/javadoc/doc-comment-spec.html#standard-tags
[MAN-C]: https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/17/
  docs/specs/man/javac.html
[MAN-D]: https://docs.oracle.com/en/java/javase/17/
  docs/specs/man/javadoc.html
