I do NOT use Outlook as my mail client for email composition and would advise you not to either. I strongly recommend Thunderbird or any client that displays plain text in properly monospace font, because I will be writing in plain text wrapped at around 64 characters, and it might look weird on, say Outlook, where every email, plain text or HTML, is displayed in proportional font, and lines FREQUENTLY get line-wrapped as they overflow.

In addition, please use PROPER email etiquette: I don't mean shallow things like starting an email with "Dear ..." or signing off with "Best Regards <name>" --- I mean the giant wall of quoted "original text" robotically appended to the end of each reply that Outlook has been mindlessly doing for DECADES. A good email client connects the replies to their original message, so most of the time including a copy of the entire message history inside EACH reply is pure noise (you may not notice this because Outlook HIDES this from you).

If you can't get your hands on a client that sends plain text emails, please at the very least DELETE the message history Outlook automatically appends to your reply: reading Outlook's disgusting, poorly formatted HTML already makes me want to vomit, so do me a favor and delete the part where it's just me talking. If you ARE replying to a specific part of my email, COPY THAT PART OUT and quote it ABOVE you reply by putting a greater-than sign ">" before each line of the quoted content. This is known as BOTTOM POSTING or IN-LINE POSTING (Wiki), and is the FAR more effective way to address specific points made by the other person than going on a monologue that only vaguely addresses every point. Here is an example (the "First Last wrote" part is optional):

First Last <flast69@example.edu> wrote on Wed, Oct XX 5:55 PM:
> I think findXXX() should throw NoSuchElementFoundException
> instead of IllegalStateException here.

Oops. That totally slipped my mind. I'll send a patched version
to you in a moment.

> You threw IllegalArgumentException in addXXX() when the list is
> full, but we should be ok since the behavior is unspecified. :)

Haha -- doesn't hurt to play it safe!

THIS is what REAL email etiquette is like. No giant quotes that keep compounding like a snowball, just what you need to follow along. The quotes are on top, so you are confronting them directly rather than hiding them, forgetting them, and being dodgy about them.
